# CIM / Listing

After diligence, the deal still isn't in market. Someone has to turn verified numbers into the story buyers read.

That document is the **CIM** — the confidential information memorandum. It's the packet a serious buyer gets under NDA: what this practice is, how it earns, where it can grow, what they're buying. **Listing** is everything that sits around it: the web blurb, the email, the one-liner that makes the right buyer ask for the CIM.

On most desks that writing is the broker, again. Thesis, growth levers, six marketing assets, a branded PDF — after they've already read the books. The file waits. Buyers wait. The listing is only as fast as that one person can write.

## What this station is

CIM / Listing is authorship plus render. One station. Verified numbers in. Narratives, listing copy, and a branded PDF out. It doesn't invent collections, seller discretionary earnings, or asking price. It writes from the sheet the broker already signed.

## What the agent does

It trusts the verified numbers. It writes the CIM body — thesis, operations, levers that are actually in the Q&A (a hygiene block that's back to full after a retirement is a lever; a hole you can't explain isn't). It drafts the listing assets from the same source so the website and the PDF don't tell two stories.

Then a renderer stamps the branded PDF. Same JSON. Same facts. Their brand file, their field map — not another firm's templates.

## Human in the loop

Asking price, multiples, and what goes to buyers under NDA stay with the broker. Nothing leaves until they sign off. The agent writes. The broker ships.

## What that unlocks

A listing that used to wait on a writing week can move when the numbers are done. The broker spends time on the call — price, story, who sees it — not on blank pages. More practices in market, same principal, without a second writer on payroll.
