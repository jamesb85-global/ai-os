# Due Diligence

A listing file arrives as a pile: three years of P&L, tax returns, a lease, payroll, a recap sheet someone typed in a hurry. Someone on the desk has to read all of it before anyone talks price. That someone is usually the broker, at night, holding two numbers that don't match and deciding which one to believe.

That's the bottleneck. Diligence is the work that makes a deal real, and it's the work that stalls every other file on the desk.

## What this station is

Due Diligence is the read. Not a valuation. Not a CIM. Source documents in, a structured review out — so the broker walks into the doctor conversation already knowing what to ask.

## What the agent does

It reads the pack. It maps who actually produces (owner, associates, hygiene). It marks add-backs only where the document supports them — owner auto on the schedule, personal line items — and leaves the rest as questions, not as smoothed earnings.

It flags what a careful buyer will flag: hygiene production down with no staffing note, a lease with no assignment clause in the copy you have, lab on the recap that doesn't match the invoices.

Then it writes the questions for the doctor. Generate and verify are separate passes. It doesn't trust the recap until the source pack agrees.

## Human in the loop

The agent never prices. What to believe, what to ask, whether this file advances — that's the broker. Findings land. A person decides.

## What that unlocks

Diligence stops being a night shift. The broker gets hours back and a cleaner yes/no. Files that shouldn't move die faster. Files that should move show up with the questions already written. That's operational leverage: more files through the same head, without skipping the read.
