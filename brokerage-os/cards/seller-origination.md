# Seller Origination

Most listings still arrive the slow way: a referral, a doctor who finally called, a name someone remembered at lunch. The rest of the territory — owners who've been licensed since the '80s, reviews slowing down, no successor on the site — sits in a spreadsheet nobody ranked this month.

The bottleneck isn't that there are no sellers. It's that finding them is a research job the broker doesn't have time to do, so the pipeline is whoever showed up.

## What this station is

Seller Origination is how the next listing shows up on purpose. It turns public information into a call list the broker can grade in minutes, not a hunt across license files and review sites.

## What the agent does

It reads the territory the way an associate would if they had a free week: license-year band, solo vs group, review velocity as a proxy for new patients, a successor already on the masthead.

Then it ranks. Call first. Watch. Succession already underway — don't treat that as a hot listing. Verify when the signal is noisy (hours look off, reviews went quiet).

The list refreshes on a cadence. Names already in the pipeline stay off it.

## Human in the loop

The list is a suggestion. Who is actually worth calling — who's a client you want, who's a flag that needs a human look — is the broker. The agent doesn't dial. It doesn't decide hot.

## What that unlocks

Origination stops depending on luck. The desk sees the territory every month. The broker spends judgment on the top of the list instead of building the list. More of the right conversations, without hiring a researcher to live in public records.
