# Content Engine

On most desks, the principal is the brand. If they don't write this week, the firm goes quiet. A webinar sits in a folder. A good idea dies in Notes. Marketing is whatever got squeezed in on Sunday night — or it doesn't happen.

That's the bottleneck: voice lives in one person's head, and there's no extra hours in the week to get it out.

## What this station is

The Content Engine is the desk's publishing rhythm. Written and video, same station, same voice. Not a separate "content person" and a separate "video person." One engine.

## What the agent does

You give it a topic, or a recording. It already has the principal's voice file — how they talk, what they won't say, the standard of the firm.

From a topic: outline first. Then the full piece. Then it breaks that piece into LinkedIn, email, a newsletter teaser — one idea, three surfaces, still their words.

From a webinar: transcript, four standalone clip briefs (hook, number, story, what to do next), captions, a YouTube pack, and a written follow-on so the session isn't a one-night event.

## Human in the loop

The point of view is the principal's. They approve the outline before a draft exists. They sign off the hooks before a clip publishes. The engine doesn't post. It drafts until someone says this sounds like us.

## What that unlocks

The firm stays in market without the principal becoming a full-time writer. A recorded hour becomes a week of assets. Capacity isn't "more posts." It's a desk that still sounds like the owner when the owner is on a listing.
