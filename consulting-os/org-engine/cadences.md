# Cadence

An operating system without a clock is just a pile of agents.

The org engine runs on a week. Same stations, same gates, so the principal isn't deciding *when* the work happens — only *whether* it cleared the bar.

**Weekday morning — Hunt.** The specialist scans for fit: pain shape, not a phone book. Cards land at the hub. The principal isn't in that loop.

**Friday morning — Keep / skip.** The hunt recap hits the human-in-the-loop gate. Keep becomes eligible for list-prep. Skip dies. Nothing promotes itself.

**Sunday — Marketing packet.** Two drafts for the week. The principal pastes when they want. The engine doesn't publish.

**Tuesday / Thursday — Paste reminder.** A nudge. Not a new post invented because a timer fired.

**Friday / Saturday / Sunday — Hub gates.** Second eyes on packaging. List quality after prep. Content quality after the packet. Quiet if it passed. Loud only if it didn't.

**Weekday evening — Handoff close.** The coordinator writes the state of the week so Monday isn't archaeology.

**Sunday afternoon — Spine.** The coordinator is the sole writer of the org's source of truth. Specialists don't fork the rules.

That's the cadence: specialists execute on a clock, the hub evaluates, the human gates the promotions. Capacity goes up because the week is designed, not because someone worked later.
